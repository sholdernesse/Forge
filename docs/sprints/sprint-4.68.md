# Sprint 4.68 — RDL Muscle Activation Guide

## Outcome

The Forge RDL guide now uses two purpose-built image sets: the original side-view sequence teaches range of motion, while a separate rear-view sequence explains which muscles drive and stabilize the movement.

## Reference reviewed

- [Barbell RDL: How to Do It, Form & Muscles Worked — Major Fitness](https://www.majorfitness.com/blogs/training-tips/barbell-rdl)

The reference uses visual muscle highlighting to make the posterior-chain emphasis easy to understand. Forge adapts that communication idea with original artwork and its own visual language; no reference artwork is copied.

## Delivered

- Preserves the approved side-view range-of-motion asset unchanged.
- Adds a separate two-panel rear-view asset with restrained muscle activation overlays.
- Uses Forge green for the primary movers: hamstrings and glutes.
- Uses muted amber for supporting stabilization: spinal erectors, lats, upper back, forearms, and core.
- Avoids red so activation is not confused with pain or injury.
- Adds a text muscle legend below every form-guide image, preserving meaning for users who cannot rely on color alone.
- Keeps movement mechanics and muscle education visually separate so neither image becomes cluttered.
- Preserves the corrected standing-to-mid-shin range, close bar path, soft knees, and neutral-spine presentation from Sprint 4.67.
- Adds regression coverage for the active asset and RDL muscle model.

## Boundaries

- Highlighting communicates broad training emphasis, not exact individual muscle recruitment or medical assessment.
- Written movement cues remain authoritative over generated imagery.
- The muscle view supplements rather than replaces the approved movement image.

## Acceptance

- Green does not cover the quadriceps, knees, or calves.
- Primary highlights remain on the visible glute and posterior hamstring line.
- Supporting highlights are visually quieter than the primary movers.
- The image and text legend communicate the same muscle hierarchy.
- Type checks, full tests, and production builds pass.
